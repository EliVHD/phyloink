    How does heterozygote fitness change the comparison?

    Compare two explicit AA/Aa/aa fitness triplets at one synthetic diploid A/a locus. Both conditions share constant N, starting A copies and horizon. Each generation weights expected random-mating genotype proportions, then samples 2N gene copies. Fitness weights are relative contributions, not survival percentages. Phenotype dominance does not determine heterozygote fitness.

    No individual genotypes, survival events or pedigrees are tracked. No mutation, migration, linked loci, changing environment, size changes, sex structure or zero-fitness extinction. Missing alleles stay absent; locus loss/fixation is not species extinction. N and weights are chosen model inputs, not estimates or observations inferred from a tree.

    For p = p(A) and q = 1−p, the post-selection frequency is (p²wAA + pqwAa)/(p²wAA + 2pqwAa + q²waa). S2’s interior no-drift equilibrium is p(A) = 2/3.     Does recovering population size restore a lost allele?

    One synthetic neutral A/a locus follows an explicit size schedule: initial N, a single temporary reduction, then one-step recovery to the initial N. A separate constant-size condition starts with the same A frequency. Each target generation resamples 2N gene copies with replacement from the previous frequency. This represents reproduction and gene-copy resampling, not a mortality cull or tracked survivors.

    No mutation, migration, selection, sex structure, linked loci, individual genotypes or pedigrees. Missing alleles cannot return through recovery alone. N is a model input, not an empirical estimate; locus loss/fixation is not species extinction. H = 2p(1−p) is gene diversity at this biallelic locus, not measured heterozygous individuals or whole-genome diversity. No observations are inferred from your tree.

    Adapt one example · model and background

    Start with N = 100, 100 A copies, bottleneck N = 5 at generations 10–19, recovery at 20, horizon 40, four trials per condition and seed 1. Inspect before, during and after the bottleneck. Change severity, timing or duration while holding other settings fixed; change seed to explore different finite outcomes. Recovery cannot recreate an absent allele. If both alleles remain, H can rise or fall in an individual run; a few traces cannot reliably estimate loss probability or guarantee an ordering against controls. Loss by the recorded final generation differs from eventual loss.

    For each target generation t, A_t is a binomial draw with 2N_t trials and probability A_(t−1)/(2N_(t−1)). This is sampling with replacement, not choosing survivors without replacement. Across repeated neutral experiments, E[p_t] stays p₀ and expected H is H₀ multiplied by (1−1/(2N_g)) for each target generation g. These are ensemble expectations, not observed genotypes or the mean of the few plotted trials.

    Follow linked alleles through one cross

    Two autosomal loci, A/a and B/b, are written as explicit chromosome pairs. AB/ab and Ab/aB both have genotype AaBb, but different phase. The slash separates homologous chromosomes; each gamete receives one allele at each locus. This synthetic model assumes equal segregation, symmetric recombination, random fertilization and complete dominance at each locus. A_ means AA or Aa; B_ means BB or Bb. Dominance does not increase transmission.

    One shared recombination fraction applies to both parents. It is not a crossover count or a physical/map distance. No sex-specific recombination, mutation, selection, epistasis, penetrance variation, environmental effects or multigeneration breeding. No genotypes are inferred from your tree.

        Frequency paths, not a forecast

        The dashed black reference is an infinite-population calculation with no drift. It is not the mean of these finite trials under selection. A few trials cannot reliably estimate fixation probability, and a rise in A does not by itself demonstrate selection. Fixation by the recorded final generation differs from eventual fixation.

        For a fixed initial frequency, larger populations have less neutral one-generation sampling variance. Any particular small set of trials can still behave differently. Gene-copy fixation at this locus is not species extinction or proof of adaptation.
